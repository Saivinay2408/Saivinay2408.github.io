Abstract

We present bootstrap-substrate, the first end-to-end computational instantiation of Carey's Quinian bootstrapping theory within a neural program-synthesis architecture. The system initializes a typed domain-specific language (DSL) with primitives from Spelke's core-knowledge systems (OBJECTS, FORMS, NUMBER, and extensions to AGENTS, PLACES, PERSONS) and runs a DreamCoder-style wake-sleep loop with Stitch anti-unification over ARC-AGI and purpose-built cross-system benchmarks. We demonstrate the properties the bootstrapping account requires. (1) Cross-system discovery: MDL compression recovers novel abstractions bridging FORMS and OBJECTS, including abs0,2, a parameterized combinator discovered on real ARC tasks (MDL savings 25, reused across 4 programs). (2) Compounding: an OBJECTS abstraction discovered in cycle 0 enables solving ARC task b9b7f026 in cycle 1, a program unreachable without the learned abstraction. (3) Outperformance of matched baselines: against a matched-cardinality Generic DSL and the VIMRL objectness-only DSL, the Spelke-initialized system solves 46/400 ARC-AGI tasks vs. 42/400 for both baselines under the original search geometry (an advantage consistent across all seeds but not statistically significant; McNemar χ²=1.3333, p=0.2482), and 50/400 vs. 42/400 under a corrected, fully disclosed search geometry (§4.15), where the re-measured advantage is significant (McNemar χ²=6.12, p=0.0133) and the control arm gains nothing. The Spelke library grows (143→171 primitives; 14 abstractions, 1 cross-system) while both baseline libraries remain static at 0 abstractions.

Sustained compounding (the staircase). On a 1530-task benchmark of 9 chains of single-operation wraps (tiers 1–5 trained, tier 6 held out), where every tier k≥2 task is machine-verified unsolvable by the virgin system, a 14-cycle run climbs from 306 to 511/1530 solved tasks, with the solve count strictly increasing over 4 consecutive cycles, the library growing from 156 to 270 primitives, and 54 cross-system abstractions discovered. The trained library transfers to a held-out 200-task test set (41 vs. 20 for a virgin library, McNemar p=9.54e-07), and beats a matched-cardinality Generic DSL across 3 seeds (McNemar p=2.7e-157). This is Carey's staircase, computationally: abstractions discovered at one rung become the parts from which the next rung is built.

The counting analog and order invariance. Two config-gated enumeration policies (per-first-argument fair shares; input-dependent-first bucket order) break the measured “int@2” search wall without special-casing any primitive, making NUMBER-anchored compositions reachable: on a machine-validated NUMBER+OBJECTS counting suite (600 train tasks, 3 chain families), the system climbs 120→494 with 5 consecutive strict increases and 12 NUMBER+OBJECTS abstractions, the counting analog of Carey's bootstrapping story. Making library compression a pure function of the solved-program set (canonical corpus order, deterministic tie-breaks) removes the curriculum-order brittleness at its mechanism: 3/3 shuffled-order seeds produce byte-identical trajectories whose final count (520) exceeds the sorted-order result (511). A blind self-generated curriculum was also tested honestly and returned a null result: both designs compound on their own tasks, but transfer to held-back compositional ladders fell below the pre-registered threshold (§5.3).

Aimed self-curriculum. The missing alignment ingredient is supplied by aimed generation: the generator conditions on unsolved target tasks' I/O examples (the task statement any learner sees, never solutions) and synthesizes its own machine-validated stepping-stone practice toward them, under a pre-registered GOAL/HIDDEN split (576/276 of the 852 held-back rungs) and a machine-checked non-equivalence protocol (every generated task checked against every target, every cycle; 53 candidates rejected). The frozen final library newly solves 27 GOAL-set rungs (pre-registered bar 25), with spillover to 16 HIDDEN-set rungs the generator never saw. Every number in this paper is machine-checked against the underlying experiment outputs by an automated verification gate.

1. Introduction

How does a mind acquire concepts that go beyond its starting vocabulary? Carey's theory of Quinian bootstrapping [4] offers a precise answer: placeholder symbols from distinct conceptual systems are first used without full interpretation, then cross-system modelling processes gradually bind them together, producing new concepts with expressive power strictly greater than any source system. The canonical example is the child's construction of the integer concept by bootstrapping from the object-tracking system (which represents small exact cardinalities) and the approximate-number system (which represents large magnitudes), with the placeholder symbols one, two, three acting as the bridge [4, 5].

Spelke's core-knowledge framework [20] provides the vocabulary of starting systems: OBJECTS (cohesion, persistence, contact), FORMS (geometry, symmetry, periodicity), NUMBER (object-tracking system, analog magnitude system), AGENTS, PERSONS, and PLACES. These are posited to be innate, domain-specific, and the raw material from which bootstrapping builds richer concepts.

Despite its theoretical prominence, Quinian bootstrapping has never been given a mechanistic computational instantiation. Prior work in program induction [8, 15, 19] learns libraries of reusable combinators but does not ground them in Spelke systems, does not track cross-system provenance, and does not claim to demonstrate the Carey bootstrapping sequence. Work in developmental AI [1, 6, 14] invokes the framework rhetorically without implementing the placeholder-binding mechanism.

We fill this gap. Our contributions are:

  1. The first Spelke-typed DSL for visual program induction. 143 primitives organized into the three active core-knowledge systems (OBJECTS, FORMS, NUMBER) with explicit system provenance tracked on every primitive.
  2. Cross-system abstraction discovery. A DreamCoder wake-sleep loop [8] with Stitch anti-unification [3] discovers cross-system abstractions bridging FORMS and OBJECTS, both on synthetic tasks (abs0,0, MDL savings 124) and on real ARC tasks (abs0,2, MDL savings 25). These are recovered by MDL compression, not heuristics.
  3. Empirical demonstration of compounding. On the Spelke Bootstrap Suite v2 (§4.3), 75 tasks unsolvable in cycle 0 become solvable in cycle 1 via three cross-system abstractions discovered in cycle 0. On ARC, task b9b7f026 is solved in cycle 1 via composition of a learned OBJECTS abstraction with OBJECTS gravity operations, a program unreachable within the enumerator's cost budget without the learned abstraction.
  4. Three-way baseline comparison. The Spelke DSL (143 prims) achieves 46/400 tasks (11.5%) on ARC-AGI versus 42/400 (10.5%) for a matched-cardinality Generic DSL and 42/400 (10.5%) for the VIMRL objectness-only DSL [1] under the original search geometry, and 50/400 (12.5%) vs. 42/400 under the corrected, disclosed geometry of §4.15, where the advantage is statistically significant (McNemar p=0.0133). Spelke discovers 14 abstractions across 3 cycles (library 143→171); both baselines discover 0. The advantage is attributable to OBJECTS-system grounding enabling cross-system program composition.
  5. Sustained multi-cycle compounding (the staircase). On a 1530-task tiered benchmark with machine-verified dependency structure, solve counts climb 306→511 across 14 cycles (4 consecutive strict increases), the library grows in 9 distinct cycles, and discovered abstractions transfer to a held-out test set including a never-trained tier (McNemar p=9.54e-07).
  6. The search wall, measured and broken (config-gated). The int@2 wall is diagnosed mechanically (constant argument combinations exhaust each root's fair bucket share before input-dependent arguments are tried) and removed by two generic, disclosed bucket policies; defaults reproduce all pre-existing results bit-identically (cross-engine differential over all flag combinations).
  7. The counting staircase. 120→494/600 over 14 cycles on machine-validated NUMBER+OBJECTS ladders with full virgin controls; 100% of tier≥2 solutions use invented abstractions by name; 12 NUMBER+OBJECTS abstractions.
  8. Order-invariant compounding. With order-invariant compression (--stitch-canonical), 3/3 shuffled-order seeds produce byte-identical trajectories with ≥3 consecutive strict increases whose final count exceeds the sorted-order result; the curriculum-order brittleness of the staircase is removed at its mechanism.
  9. Self-generated curriculum, tested honestly. Two materially distinct frontier designs (typed stochastic band sampling; abstraction- targeted growth); machine-validated self-tasks; transfer measured on held-back compositional ladders never trained on.
  10. Aimed self-curriculum (goal-directed generation). A proximity-guided generator that builds its own ladder toward tasks it cannot yet solve, conditioning only on target I/O examples under a pre-registered split and per-cycle machine-checked non-equivalence: 27 GOAL-set rungs newly solvable (pre-registered bar 25), with spillover to 16 HIDDEN-set rungs the generator never saw.
  11. ARC re-measured under the corrected machinery. 46→50/400 with the generic control unchanged at 42/400 (canonical protocol, both arms re-run, configuration disclosed before launch); the relative advantage reaches statistical significance.

2. Background and Related Work

2.1 Quinian Bootstrapping

Carey [4] defines Quinian bootstrapping as a process in which placeholder symbols (terms used before their meaning is fully determined) are integrated across incommensurable representational systems through analogical and structural mapping processes. The resulting concept is not reducible to either source system: the integer concept is neither pure cardinality-tracking nor pure magnitude estimation, but a new structure that emerges from their coupling. Spelke and colleagues [20, 5] provide the empirical base: infant studies demonstrate that the six core-knowledge systems are present from birth, domain-specific, and computation-ready, making them the natural starting vocabulary for any bootstrapping account.

2.2 Program Synthesis and Library Learning

Ellis et al. [8] introduced DreamCoder, a wake-sleep algorithm for program induction: a wake phase solves tasks using a recognition network to guide enumeration; an abstraction sleep phase compresses the solved-program corpus into a growing library; a dreaming sleep phase trains the recognition network on imagined programs. The library grows monotonically, and each cycle's abstractions become first-class primitives in the next. Stitch [3] provides a scalable anti-unification algorithm that finds the MDL-optimal library given a corpus, replacing the slower compression step in the original DreamCoder.

The ARC-AGI benchmark [6] (Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus) presents 400 training tasks, each a small set of input-output grid pairs, designed to require core-knowledge reasoning without large-data pretraining. It has become the standard evaluation for human-like abstraction. Competitive approaches include hand-crafted DSLs [11], learned program synthesis [16], and large language models with tool use [21]; none are grounded in the developmental cognitive science literature.

2.3 Cognitive-AI Alignment

Ainooson et al. [1] present VIMRL, an objectness-centric DSL for ARC with 45 primitives, the closest prior work in spirit. They do not implement number or forms primitives, do not run a library- learning loop, and do not relate their results to Carey's bootstrapping. Lake et al. [14] propose Building Machines That Learn and Think Like People but focus on model-based reinforcement learning. Our work is the first to translate the specific Carey-Spelke theoretical framework into working software with measurable predictions.

3. Method

3.1 The Spelke-Typed DSL

We define a simply-typed lambda calculus over ARC grids. Base types are grid, object, color, int, bool, list[·], and point. Every primitive carries a system tag ∈ {OBJECTS, FORMS, NUMBER, GLUE} inherited from its Spelke provenance. The full library contains 143 typed primitives:

SystemPrimsRepresentative primitives
OBJECTS38 extract_objects, obj_largest, render_object, gravity ×4, fill_interior
FORMS31 rotate90/180/270, flip_h/v, transpose, extract_tile, scale_up/down
NUMBER41 count_objects, obj_size, OTS cap-3, ANS Weber ratio
GLUE33 compose, if_then_else, map, fold, analogy alignment

Table 1. Spelke-typed DSL primitive counts by system. GLUE includes generic higher-order combinators and the cross-domain analogy primitive.

Each primitive's type signature is enforced during enumeration: the type-directed enumerator [8] only constructs well-typed programs, which radically prunes the search space for cross-type compositions.

3.2 Wake-Sleep Architecture

Figure 1. Wake-sleep architecture. The Spelke-typed DSL (143 primitives across OBJECTS, FORMS, NUMBER, and GLUE) feeds the AST solver; Stitch discovers shared structure across solved programs; discovered abstractions become first-class primitives in subsequent cycles. The cross-system abstraction box (red) is the Carey signature: it emerges from the compression step, not from any prior over cross-system compositions.

Wake phase. For each task, the system first attempts 22 hand-coded AST-producing strategies organized by Spelke system (single-primitive FORMS transforms, OBJECTS pipelines, color operations, gravity, symmetry completion, scaling, tiling, and five cross-system strategies including FORMS-on-extracted-object and NUMBER-recolor). Strategies return typed program ASTs (not opaque closures), so the compression step can decompose their structure. Tasks unsolved after all strategies pass to the type-directed enumerator with a cost budget of 5 and a 5-second per-task time limit.

Abstraction sleep. The corpus of solved-task programs is compressed by the Stitch anti-unifier [3]. Stitch runs pairwise anti-unification over program ASTs, replacing differing subtrees with typed holes. The MDL objective accepts an abstraction if it reduces total description length:

ΔL = size(pattern) × (count − 1) − count − nholes > 0

Accepted abstractions are registered as callable primitives with concrete type signatures (so the enumerator can treat them as any other primitive in the next cycle) and deduplication prevents re-invention of existing patterns.

System-provenance tracking. Every abstraction tracks systems_composed: the union of Spelke-system tags from all primitives in its body, plus the systems of primitives that were generalized into holes (since holes represent the caller's contribution). This is the key fix required to detect the Carey signature: a hole-bearing abstraction that abstracts away a FORMS operation over an OBJECTS pipeline reports systems_composed = {FORMS, OBJECTS} even though the FORMS primitive does not literally appear in the abstract body.

Dreaming sleep. A DreamGenerator constructs synthetic programs by three modes: (30%) pipeline dreams that string OBJECTS primitives; (30%) cross-system dreams that deliberately compose OBJECTS with FORMS; and (40%) standard PCFG sampling. These programs train a two-layer NumPy MLP recognition network (50 → 128 → nprims with He initialization, ReLU, sigmoid output, binary cross-entropy loss) to predict which primitives appear in a solution given the task's input-output pairs encoded as a 50-dimensional feature vector.

3.3 The Baselines

We compare against two baselines. The Generic DSL (143 primitives) uses the same architecture, strategies, enumeration budget, and Stitch compressor but with flat, non-modular primitive organization: 143 generic list/grid operations without Spelke system provenance tags. This is matched in cardinality to Spelke. The VIMRL DSL (45 primitives, Ainooson et al. [1]) is an objectness-only DSL covering segmentation and spatial operations but without FORMS geometry or NUMBER arithmetic modules. We use VIMRL as it represents the closest prior cognitive-AI approach to ARC-AGI program synthesis.

4. Experiments

4.1 Task Sets

The core mechanism claims (§4.2–4.6) are evaluated on three task sets (420 tasks total), described here. The purpose-built compositional benchmarks, namely the 875-task Spelke Bootstrap Suite v2 (§4.3), the 1530-task staircase suite (§4.10), the 600-task counting suite (§4.11), and the machine-generated self-curriculum pools (§4.13–4.14), are described in their respective sections; every tier-k≥2 task in the compositional suites is machine-verified unsolvable by the virgin system.

ARC-AGI training set (400 tasks). The standard benchmark [6]. Each task has 3–6 training input-output pairs and 1–3 test pairs.

Synthetic cross-system tasks (15 tasks). We generate ARC-format tasks requiring programs of the form (T (render_object (obj_largest (extract_objects input)))) where T ∈ {rotate90, rotate180, rotate270, flip_h, flip_v}, 3 instances each. Tasks use L-, T-, J-, S-, C-shaped objects with distinct coloring so that (a) the correct output is not achievable by any single FORMS primitive applied to the full input grid, and (b) no existing AST-solver strategy other than the cross-system strategy _try_form_on_extracted_object can solve them. These tasks are included in both cycles to allow the system to discover the cross-system abstraction.

Compounding tasks (5 tasks). Tasks of the form (extract_tile (render_object (obj_largest (extract_objects input)))) where the input contains a solid-color rectangular object (so extract_tile returns its 1×1 color tile) and a single-pixel marker of a different color. These are withheld from cycle 0: they are presented only in cycle 1, after the OBJECTS-pipeline abstraction abs0,1 has been discovered and registered. No cycle-0 strategy reaches extract_tile composed with the OBJECTS pipeline.

Statistical Analysis

McNemar's test yields χ²=1.3333, p=0.2482 for Spelke vs. Generic across 5 seeds (N=400 tasks per seed). The +4 task advantage (46 vs. 42) is consistent across all 5 seeds but does not reach conventional significance (p<0.05) at this scale. We interpret this as a power limitation: detecting a ~1% solve-rate difference requires approximately 1500+ tasks under McNemar's test. Sample efficiency curves (Table 7) provide complementary monotonic evidence for the advantage. Two adequately-powered follow-ups resolve the question: the 1530-task comparison of §4.10 (p=2.7e-157), and the re-measurement of this exact protocol under the corrected search geometry in §4.15, where the advantage doubles to +8 (50 vs. 42) and reaches significance (p=0.0133).

Figure 3. Three-way comparison on 400 ARC-AGI tasks. Left: solve rate (%). Right: number of unique abstractions discovered across 3 wake-sleep cycles. Spelke discovers 14 abstractions (including 1 cross-system); both baselines discover 0.

The Generic baseline discovers zero abstractions across all three cycles: without Spelke-system provenance tracking, the compressor finds no recurring multi-primitive patterns worth compressing. The VIMRL baseline, limited to 45 objectness primitives, similarly discovers zero abstractions. This confirms that FORMS/NUMBER module structure is critical for library learning, not just task solving.

4.5 ARC-Native Cross-System Discovery and Compounding

Beyond the synthetic demonstration (§4.2–4.3), the canonical 400-task ARC run produces both cross-system discovery and compounding on real ARC tasks.

Cross-system abstraction on ARC. In cycle 0, abs0,2 emerges with systems_composed = {FORMS, OBJECTS} and MDL savings of 25, reused across 4 programs. This is a parameterized abstraction discovered without access to synthetic tasks; the system found the FORMS+OBJECTS pattern in naturally-occurring ARC task solutions. MDL savings are lower than in the synthetic setting (25 vs. 124) because fewer ARC tasks exhibit cross-system structure, consistent with ARC's single-concept design [6].

4.8 Curriculum Pre-training with Cross-System Tasks

We construct a curriculum of 120 synthetic ARC-format tasks spanning four cross-system patterns: NUMBER+OBJECTS (30 tasks, canvas sizes 8×8–15×15): count N same-colored objects, render as 1×N colored row using count_objects; FORMS+OBJECTS+PLACES (30 tasks, 8×8): extract largest object, apply geometric transform (rotate/flip), place in target quadrant; NUMBER+OBJECTS replicate (30 tasks, canvas sizes 8×8–15×15): extract largest object, tile it N times horizontally where N = total object count; count_cells (30 tasks, 3×3–4×4): N non-zero cells arranged in a connected region, render as 1×N colored row using count_cells; this pattern covers ARC tasks where the count is of total pixels rather than connected components, and directly matches ARC task d631b094. Task sizes span the ARC distribution to encourage size-invariant abstractions.

Cross-system abstraction discovery. After one curriculum pre-training cycle over 140 tasks (120 cross-system + 15 synthetic cross-system + 5 compounding), the system discovers 10 cross-system abstractions (of 15 total): 8 NUMBER+OBJECTS, 1 FORMS+OBJECTS, and 1 FORMS+OBJECTS+PLACES. The top NUMBER+OBJECTS abstractions have MDL savings of 434, 434, 405, 405, and 376 bits, all with is_cross_system = True; the Carey signature holds at the primitive level. 4.10 The Staircase: Sustained Multi-Cycle Compounding

Sections 4.3–4.5 demonstrated single steps of compounding: an abstraction discovered in one cycle unlocks tasks in the next. The bootstrapping account requires more. Carey's staircase is iterated: each round of abstractions becomes the substrate for the next. Every previous run flatlined after one such step. This section tests sustained climbing directly.

Benchmark. We construct spelke_staircase: 9 chains of compositional tasks in 6 tiers, where each tier-k task applies exactly one additional operation to its chain's tier-(k−1) transform (e.g. chain B: a double-gravity composite at tier 1, wrapped in make_border at tier 2, flip_h at tier 3, …). The training set has 1530 tasks (tiers 1–5); 200 test tasks include tier 6, which never appears in training. The dependency structure is machine-validated, not assumed: a pre-flight harness verifies every rung (tier 1 solvable by the virgin enumerator at max_cost=4 but not at max_cost=2; every tier k≥2 family unsolvable virgin and solvable with the designed tier-(k−1) abstraction hand-registered), and the verification gate re-confirms on the full benchmark that the virgin system solves zero tier-k≥2 tasks by AST search or by enumeration at the experiment budget.

Protocol (and what we disable). In staircase mode the AST solver is restricted to its generic library_chains strategy (composing library functions); all hand-written task-type templates are off, so every solve is attributable to search plus the learned library, not to template coverage. Enumeration runs at max_cost=4 with a 3 s budget, fair_caps (each root primitive receives an equal share of each type bucket) and cap_scale=8; both settings are disclosed because the staircase's reachability geometry was designed against them. Neural recognition is off. Under this protocol the enumerator-engagement condition is satisfied by construction (with templates disabled, enumeration and library-chain composition are the only solvers), so we report it as a design property, not a finding: 40.5% of tasks newly solved after cycle 0 were solved by the enumerator.

Result: the staircase climbs. Table 8 and Figure 7 show the 14-cycle trajectory. The system enters at 306/1530 (tier-1 coverage by the virgin enumerator), and climbs to 511/1530, with the per-cycle solve count strictly increasing for 4 consecutive cycles and the library growing in 9 distinct cycles, from 156 to 270 primitives (114 invented abstractions, 54 of them cross-system). Unlocks proceed up the tiers as the account predicts: tier 2 first solved in cycle 1; tier 3 first solved in cycle 3; tier 4 first solved in cycle 3; tier 5 first solved in cycle 3. Of all solved tier-k≥2 tasks, 100.0% (205/205) use at least one invented abstraction by name in their solution program.

4.11 Breaking the Search Wall: the Counting Staircase

The staircase suite of §4.10 exercises OBJECTS+FORMS crossings only because NUMBER-anchored compositions were unreachable (the "int@2 wall"). We diagnosed the wall mechanically (probe_int_wall.py): under fair-caps enumeration, a multi-argument primitive's bucket share is consumed by constant argument combinations (literal×literal) and by its earliest first-arguments, before structured, input-dependent arguments are ever paired. Two config-gated bucket policies remove this without special-casing any primitive: fair_args (each first-argument candidate gets a guaranteed slice of the root's share, denominated by budget-feasible candidates only) and dep_first (input-dependent candidates precede constants in every bucket). 4.12 Order Robustness via Order-Invariant Compression

The deepest brittleness in the staircase result: under shuffled task order, compounding never starts. Code-level diagnosis located the mechanism precisely: the anti-unification pass mines only the first 50 corpus programs (corpus order = task order), and ranking ties break by corpus position; shuffled corpora hand the top of the ranking to hole-bearing skeletal patterns whose expansions crowd the enumeration buckets. The fix is structural, not a tuning patch: --stitch-canonical makes compression a pure function of the solved-program set (corpus content-sorted by canonical body serialization, with task metadata deliberately excluded so that tier labels cannot leak back in, and deterministic tie-breaks). Since per-task solving is order-independent, the entire pipeline becomes presentation-order-invariant up to enumeration-budget jitter; a unit test proves permutation invariance of compression exactly.

4.14 Aimed Self-Curriculum: Goal-Directed Generation

§4.13's mechanism analysis named the missing ingredient: nothing aligned where the self-curriculum practices with what the system cannot yet do. We supply that alignment in the way failure-driven practice works for a child: the generator is shown the task statements (I/O example pairs) of unsolved target tasks, never solutions (to which it has no access), and synthesizes practice toward them. Each cycle, for a rotating sample of unsolved GOAL targets, a proximity-guided beam search composes applications of the current library over the target's training inputs, scored by a generic shape-and-cell-agreement metric; beam prefixes that strictly improve proximity become practice programs, and each practice task is that program executed on the same generic random-input distributions as §4.13, never on target inputs. The pool is topped up with §4.13's band sampling, and the engine trains exactly as before.

Integrity protocol (pre-registered in full before the run): the 852 held-back rungs were split 576 GOAL / 276 HIDDEN (stratified, seeded, sha256-pinned) with the split disclosed before launch; the generator may condition only on GOAL examples; a non-equivalence protocol machine-checks every generated task against every target (exact I/O duplicate, ≥90% cell-agreement near-duplicate, and verbatim-input checks) every cycle before training, and the verifier re-runs the full check on the persisted pools post-hoc (53 candidates rejected across the run); evaluation uses the frozen final library under standard search. Virgin controls solve 0 of the targets.

Over 8 cycles the loop compounded on its own stepping stones (69→680 cumulative self-solves, 51 abstractions invented; from cycle 3 onward the beam fully reached 1–4 of its conditioned targets per cycle). The frozen library then newly solves 27/576 GOAL rungs (4.7%) and, as spillover the generator never aimed at, 16/276 HIDDEN rungs (5.8%), versus 19/852 (2.2%) for the best blind design of §4.13. Twelve of the 27 solved GOAL rungs were never conditioned on (sibling-family generalization from stepping-stone abstractions), and the HIDDEN spillover rate (5.8%) more than doubles the blind band rate (2.2%) on tasks the generator never saw, evidence that the alignment mechanism, not target exposure, does the work. Verdict: goal-directed self-curriculum works: 27 GOAL-set rungs newly solvable (pre-registered bar 25), with spillover to 16 HIDDEN-set rungs the generator never saw. 5. Discussion

5.1 The Carey Signature

Carey's account requires three things: (i) placeholder symbols from distinct systems; (ii) cross-system modelling processes that compose them; (iii) the resulting concept has expressive power exceeding either source. We assess each using the cross-system abstractions discovered in our experiments.

(i) Placeholders. The hole ●1 in abs0,0 (synthetic, MDL savings 124) and abs0,2 (ARC-native, MDL savings 25) is exactly a Quinean placeholder: a typed slot (type grid→grid) that the system uses before fixing its value. The hole ranges over the full space of FORMS transforms, a kind of intentional indeterminacy fundamental to the bootstrapping account.

(ii) Cross-system composition. The body of both abstractions combines the OBJECTS pipeline (extract_objects → obj_largest/obj_smallest → render_object) with a FORMS placeholder. Neither system can express this on its own: OBJECTS lacks geometric transforms; FORMS has no notion of object individuation. The composition is the cross-system modelling process.

(iii) Expressive power. The compounding results (§4.3, §4.5) provide the operational definition. When OBJECTS pipeline abstractions become first-class primitives, the system reaches programs of previously inaccessible depth. On synthetic tasks (§4.1), extract_tile(abs0,1(input)) becomes reachable: extract_tile is a FORMS primitive applied to an OBJECTS abstraction, a depth-2 program whose underlying OBJECTS pipeline would otherwise exceed the cost budget; this is the cross-system composition Carey predicts. On ARC (§4.5), task b9b7f026 is solved in cycle 1 via (abs0,10 (gravity_right (gravity_down input))): an OBJECTS-system composition that is unreachable as a depth-3 program in cycle 0 but becomes depth-2 once abs0,10 is registered as a cost-1 primitive. Both cases satisfy condition (c): at least one task becomes solvable in the next cycle that was structurally inaccessible before.

5.2 Cross-System Structure in ARC

A finding with theoretical significance: of 400 ARC training tasks, only one naturally produces a program that combines FORMS and OBJECTS system primitives. ARC tasks are designed as clean, single-concept puzzles [6], and this design constraint limits cross-system opportunity. The canonical run discovers one cross-system abstraction on ARC (abs0,2, MDL savings 25, reuse 4), demonstrating that the mechanism works on real data, but the signal is sparse. This has implications for benchmark design: if the goal is to test Quinian bootstrapping at scale, evaluation requires tasks with richer cross-system structure.

5.3 Limitations and Honest Assessment

Statistical power and the ARC advantage. Under the original search geometry, the +4 task advantage (46 vs. 42 tasks, 11.5% vs. 10.5%) is not statistically significant at conventional thresholds (McNemar χ²=1.3333, p=0.2482): the 400-task ARC benchmark is underpowered for detecting 1% solve-rate differences. That caveat is resolved in two ways, each with its own scope. At adequate power (the 1530-task suite, §4.10), the advantage is decisive (p=2.7e-157) but on a constructed benchmark. At the original 400-task scale, the re-measurement under the corrected, disclosed search geometry (§4.15) doubles the advantage to +8 (50 vs. 42) and reaches significance (χ²=6.12, p=0.0133), but only under those disclosed flags; the original-geometry result stands as stated for the default configuration, and the absolute solve rate (12.5%) remains far below the human baseline.

Staircase scope and protocol. Four design choices bound the staircase claim. (1) The suite exercises OBJECTS+FORMS crossings only: NUMBER-anchored tiers are unreachable under the enumerator's bucket geometry (the int@2 wall), so the staircase says nothing about NUMBER-system bootstrapping. (2) In staircase mode the AST solver is restricted to the generic library_chains strategy; this strengthens attribution (no template can solve a task) but means the absolute solve rates are not comparable to template-enabled runs. (3) Enumeration uses fair_caps and cap_scale=8, diversity-preserving cap settings the tiers were designed against; defaults reproduce all pre-staircase results. (4) The enumerator-share condition is satisfied by construction (templates are off), so we do not present enumerator engagement as an empirical finding of this experiment.

Scope of the search-wall, counting, and order results. (1) The int@2 wall and the missing counting story are resolved by §4.11, but only under the config-gated fair_args/dep_first policies; the default geometry retains the wall, and the counting suite, like the staircase suite, is designed-to-be-climbable (it proves the engine can climb a NUMBER+OBJECTS ladder, not that such ladders arise naturally). (2) Curriculum-order brittleness is resolved at its mechanism by §4.12 (--stitch-canonical), config-gated and disclosed; order-invariance is exact for compression and empirical for the full pipeline (enumeration-budget jitter under wall-clock budgets remains a seed-level variance source). (3) The blind self-curriculum result is reported exactly as measured in §4.13 (null result); the held-back transfer sets are human-designed ladders, so transfer measures whether self-invented abstractions overlap human-designed compositional structure. (4) All new mechanisms are OFF by default; every pre-existing number reproduces under default flags, and every new number traces to experiments/outputs/ via automated verification gates (verify_paper_v6.py and successors).

Scope of the aimed-curriculum and re-measured ARC results. (1) The aimed generator conditions on GOAL-set task statements; the integrity line (conditioning on examples is legitimate, importing targets is not) is enforced mechanically (per-cycle non-equivalence checks, re-run post-hoc by the gate) rather than by trust. HIDDEN-set spillover (16/276) measures what aiming buys beyond distributional overlap. (2) The targets remain human-designed ladder rungs: aimed self-curriculum demonstrates alignment with an external compositional domain, not open-ended discovery of natural tasks; the GOAL solve rate (27/576, 4.7%) also means most rungs remain out of reach. (3) The ARC re-measurement is reported exactly as observed (the new machinery moves ARC: 46→50/400); all new mechanisms remain config-gated and OFF by default, and every earlier number reproduces under its disclosed flags.

AGENTS and PLACES primitives. The 170-primitive full Spelke DSL achieves 45/400 (11.2%) vs. 46/400 (11.5%) for 143-primitive Spelke. AGENTS and PLACES primitives do not fire on the ARC training distribution, which encodes single-concept tasks by design [6]. This is a null result and an expected limitation of the benchmark scope.

Synthetic task design. The 15 cross-system and 5 compounding tasks (§4.1) and the 875-task Spelke Bootstrap Suite v2 (§4.3) were designed by the authors to exhibit the target phenomenon. This is correct experimental methodology (one cannot test bootstrapping without tasks that require it), but limits external validity. A key open question is whether naturally-occurring visual reasoning benchmarks contain sufficient cross-system structure to replicate these results.

Enumerator speed. All post-staircase experiments use a Rust enumeration engine (persistent worker, cached enumeration tables, in-Rust candidate evaluation with memoized DAG sharing) measuring 133.8× total / 607.6× median over the Python engine on 40 ARC tasks at max_cost=6 with a 2 s budget (idle machine, 2026-06-11), with zero Python-only solves and a 30,093-comparison differential test showing zero evaluation mismatches; every Rust-claimed solution is re-verified in Python before acceptance. This still trails the original DreamCoder Rust enumerator, which uses task-parallel enumeration, PCFG-guided top-down search, and bitset memoization. Cross-system programs at depth ≥ 6 remain unreachable within practical budgets. The remaining gap is algorithmic: an engineering limitation, not a theoretical one.

Baseline fairness. The Generic baseline uses a matched-cardinality DSL (143 primitives) without Spelke module structure. The VIMRL baseline uses 45 objectness primitives following Ainooson et al. [1]. The Spelke vs. Generic solve-rate gap (+1.0pp) is modest; the more significant gap is in library growth: 14 abstractions vs. 0. The VIMRL parity with Generic on solve rate but zero abstractions confirms that FORMS/NUMBER module structure is critical for library learning, not just task solving.

Plateaus and open-ended growth. Multi-cycle compounding is demonstrated (§4.10–4.11), but every run converges to a fixed point well short of its ceiling (511/1530 on the staircase; 494/600 on the counting suite): once the solve set freezes, the compression corpus freezes, the library freezes, and the enumeration table (a function of the library) returns the identical solve set forever. Compounding engages under tier-ordered curricula, order-invariant compression, or aimed generation, but open-ended growth on a frozen corpus has not been shown, and whether staircase structure arises in natural task distributions remains the central open question (on ARC the cross-system signal is sparse, §5.2).

5.4 Relation to Prior Work

DreamCoder [8] demonstrates library learning on list processing, string editing, and scene understanding but does not ground primitives in Spelke systems and does not measure cross-system provenance. Stitch [3] provides the efficient anti-unification backbone we build on. VIMRL [1] is the closest cognitive-AI predecessor but lacks FORMS/NUMBER systems and library learning. Chollet's ARC paper [6] explicitly calls for systems grounded in core knowledge; this work is a direct response to that call.

6. Conclusion

We have presented the first computational instantiation of Carey's Quinian bootstrapping theory within a neural program-synthesis system. By initializing the DSL with Spelke's core-knowledge systems, running a DreamCoder wake-sleep loop with Stitch compression, and tracking cross-system provenance through every abstraction, we demonstrate that:

  1. The system spontaneously discovers abs0,2, a FORMS+OBJECTS combinator with MDL savings 25 on real ARC tasks, and abs0,0 with savings 124 on synthetic cross-system tasks, both matching the Carey signature of a placeholder spanning incommensurable systems.
  2. Compounding is sustained, not single-step. Beyond the single-cycle demonstrations (75 Tier 3 tasks unlocked on the Spelke Bootstrap Suite v2, §4.3; task b9b7f026 on ARC, §4.5), the staircase run climbs 306→511/1530 over 14 cycles with 54 cross-system abstractions and transfers to a never-trained tier (§4.10), and, once the measured search wall is broken, the counting staircase climbs 120→494/600 with NUMBER+OBJECTS abstraction towers, the computational analog of Carey's integer construction (§4.11). With order-invariant compression the climb is exactly invariant to task presentation order (§4.12).
  3. The Spelke-initialized system beats matched baselines wherever the comparison is adequately powered: 50/400 vs. 42/400 on ARC-AGI under the corrected, disclosed search geometry (McNemar χ²=6.12, p=0.0133; §4.15), against 46/400 vs. 42/400, not significant (p=0.2482), under the original geometry (§4.4); and 306+ vs. 68 per seed on the 1530-task suite (p=2.7e-157; §4.10). Spelke libraries grow in every experiment; both baseline libraries remain static at 0 abstractions.
  4. The system can build its own ladder. Conditioned only on the I/O examples of tasks it cannot solve, under a pre-registered GOAL/HIDDEN split and machine-checked non-equivalence, aimed self-generated practice makes 27/576 GOAL rungs and 16/276 never-seen HIDDEN rungs newly solvable (§4.14), where blind self-curricula fall below threshold (§4.13).

Quinian bootstrapping is no longer purely a verbal theory. It is a measurable property of a running system, with an operational definition: an abstraction bootstraps if and only if it (a) combines primitives from at least two distinct Spelke systems, (b) was not given to the system but was recovered by MDL compression, and (c) enables solving at least one task in the next cycle that was unsolvable in the prior cycle. All three conditions are met here, repeatedly, across designed benchmarks, a natural benchmark, and self-generated curricula, with every number machine-checked against the underlying experiment outputs.

Future directions include: closing the gap between aimed practice and full target coverage (27 of 576 GOAL rungs is a beginning, not an arrival); finding or constructing naturally occurring task distributions with staircase structure; escaping the fixed-point plateaus by coupling the generator and solver more tightly; task-parallel, PCFG-guided enumeration to reach programs at depth ≥ 6; integrating language conditioning into the wake-sleep loop; and applying the provenance-tracking machinery to large language models whose implicit knowledge systems remain largely unmapped.
